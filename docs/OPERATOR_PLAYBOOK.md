# Infrastructure Operator Playbook

## Ideal counterparty

An operating company or holding group with several of the following:

- profitable or financeable operating cashflow;
- warehouse, commercial property or underused technical space;
- solar, storage, energy expertise or material power consumption;
- fragmented ERP, shop, CRM, document and reporting workflows;
- a principal who can approve data access, budget and operating change;
- repeatable internal workloads before external compute ambitions.

## Qualification

Reject or defer when:

- the only thesis is cheap electricity or rising GPU demand;
- no one can provide an accepted baseline;
- the operator is expected to work indefinitely in return for vague future upside;
- the asset company expects control over unrelated operator IP or ventures;
- core property collateral is proposed before proven workload;
- the parties want a JV because they have not priced services, assets or risk.

## Sales motion

1. Diagnostic conversation with the principal.
2. Sell the €6,500 Partnership Blueprint.
3. Open a controlled data room.
4. Compile the investment and operating decision pack.
5. Route legal, tax, finance, insurance and energy issues to specialists.
6. Close a bounded pilot with separate third-party costs.
7. Procure against accepted workload and tests.
8. Integrate read-first and operate in shadow mode.
9. Accept production authority by workflow.
10. Prove value and expand only at G6.

## Commercial boundaries

- Phase 0 is fixed scope and fixed fee.
- Implementation is a separate SOW.
- Hardware, cloud, energy, lawyers, advisers and lenders contract transparently.
- Managed operations have a capacity and escalation envelope.
- Performance fees are capped and tied to auditable value categories.
- Capacity or host rights have their own instrument.
- No implied equity, exclusivity, board right, first refusal or claim on unrelated IP.

## Compounding asset

Every client engagement should produce reusable, de-identified improvements to:

- qualification and underwriting skills;
- canonical data schemas;
- contract issue maps;
- connector and workflow patterns;
- acceptance tests and runbooks;
- evidence and value-ledger standards;
- reference architectures by sector and site type.
