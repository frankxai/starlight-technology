# Starlight Infrastructure Partnership OS

## Product thesis

The unit of work is not a server, solar array or AI agent. It is an investable operating agreement that binds five systems:

1. parties and authority;
2. workloads and economic value;
3. physical assets and energy;
4. software, models and operations;
5. contracts, finance and evidence.

A productive node exists only when all five are coherent. The default first transaction is a customer-funded productivity node: the operating company buys capability for its own named workflows, while the AI operator implements and manages the reusable intelligence layer. Hosted capacity, offtake-backed finance and a dedicated NodeCo are later structures, not opening assumptions.

## Parties

| Party | Contribution | Receives | Must never be implied |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset / operating company | site, power, data, workflow, equipment or capital | operating improvement, owned assets, bounded service | ownership of operator IP or unrelated ventures |
| AI operator | architecture, runtime, skills, integration and operations | fees, contracted capacity, reusable proof | unlimited access, internal-employee status or client equity |
| Bank / leasing partner | debt, lease or payment structure | repayment, security and reporting | technical or operating responsibility |
| Energy / EPC partner | PV, storage, metering, tariff and O&M | project revenue and service obligations | compute utilization or AI outcome risk |
| Technology supplier | equipment, warranty and support | purchase or lease consideration | control over business data or workloads |
| Counsel / tax / insurance | specialist validation | professional fees and reliance boundaries | general approval of the whole investment thesis |

## Deal models

1. **Customer-funded productivity node** — default. Internal workflows and company value justify the purchase.
2. **Hosted capacity agreement** — site and power are contracted independently from compute ownership and utilization risk.
3. **Financed digital transformation** — the operating company borrows or leases for a defined productive programme.
4. **Offtake-backed node** — minimum committed workload supports capacity expansion.
5. **NodeCo / project company** — only after one node has proven utilization, controls, economics and exit.

## Seven gates

- **G0 Mandate** — principals, scope, authority, exclusions and data-room protocol.
- **G1 Baseline** — accepted operational, workload, IT, energy and financial evidence.
- **G2 Underwriting** — selected deal structure, sources and uses, contract map and downside.
- **G3 Pilot authority** — signed SOW, hard budget, acceptance tests and human approval matrix.
- **G4 Shadow operation** — dual-run evidence before production write access.
- **G5 Production acceptance** — verified value, incidents, ownership and exit artefacts.
- **G6 Expansion** — utilization, offtake and debt-service case justify additional capital.

## Commercial product line

Fixed-scope Phase 0, targeted for 15 business days after complete inputs:

- party, asset and workload graph;
- current IT and trust-boundary map;
- accepted baseline and value pools;
- three viable deal structures;
- contract and specialist issue matrix;
- sources-and-uses and downside model;
- procurement and integration sequence;
- risk register and 90-day pilot mandate.

No hardware, financing, legal opinion, tax opinion, production credentials or unlimited implementation is included.

### Pilot implementation — indicative €24,000–€75,000

Three bounded workstreams, accepted architecture, separate third-party costs and staged production authority.

### Managed operator — indicative €3,500–€15,000 monthly

Node operations, workflow improvement, evidence, vendor coordination, reporting and capacity governance inside a named service envelope.

## Internal investment gates

These are planning controls, not promises or lender terms:

- no hardware before named workload and accountable owner;
- no initial pilot without an executable stop and portability path;
- target payback for the bounded first pilot: at most 18 months;
- do not count working-capital release as recurring P&L;
- do not secure speculative AI workload with core property;
- do not form a JV or grant equity to postpone pricing or ownership decisions;
- expansion debt follows contracted or accepted cashflow evidence.
